The Scenario: Equipment Alert Response
Read through this workflow before sorting anything. You'll see a realistic process — a familiar one from this course — and decide where AI fits in and where humans need to make the calls.

🏭 Situation

A manufacturing facility uses an AI-assisted monitoring system on its production floor. When equipment behavior changes, the system detects it and the team responds. The process involves several steps — from first detection all the way through to resolution and documentation.

📋 The Steps in This Process

Here are eight steps in this workflow. In the next phase, you'll sort each one into AI helps here or Human decides here — based on what you've learned about what AI does well and where human judgment is essential.

1 Continuously scan sensor data across all stations for anomalies
2 Decide whether a flagged reading is a real problem or normal system variation
3 Group related alerts and suggest the most likely root cause based on historical patterns
4 Confirm whether the AI's suggested cause matches what maintenance actually finds
5 Approve or adjust the proposed repair action before work begins
6 Generate a draft incident report from system logs and alert data
7 Review, correct, and sign off on the incident report before it's filed
8 Track patterns across multiple incidents over time to flag recurring issues

The Assembled Workflow
Here's how this process actually flows — alternating between where AI handles the work and where humans make the decisions. Notice how they hand off to each other.
What this shows

AI and humans don't work in separate silos — they alternate. AI handles the high-volume, pattern-based steps. Humans step in at every point where interpretation, judgment, or accountability is required. Neither can do the other's job effectively in this workflow.

What This Activity Showed

✓

AI doesn't run a workflow from start to finish — it handles specific steps where speed and pattern recognition add value, then hands off to humans for interpretation and decisions.

✓

Human decision points aren't inefficiencies — they're where accountability lives. Removing them doesn't make a workflow faster, it makes it riskier.

✓

There's no single correct workflow design. The principles are consistent — map where AI helps, define where humans decide — but how they're applied depends on the organization's context, risks, and responsibilities.
