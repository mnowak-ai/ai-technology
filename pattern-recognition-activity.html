📈 The Trends in This Data

Morning ramp-up (hours 1–8): Response time climbs gradually each morning as more employees come online and traffic increases.
Overnight recovery (hours 18–24): When traffic drops overnight, response time falls steadily back toward the baseline.
Second-day repeat (hours 25–40): The same morning ramp-up happens again the next day — confirming this is a regular pattern, not a random event.

⚠️ The Anomalies in This Data

Hour 14 spike (1,840ms): A sudden jump to 5× the normal response time — far outside the regular trend. This is the biggest red flag.
Hour 31 spike (1,240ms): A second spike the following day — same time window, similar pattern. Two similar spikes at the same time of day suggests a recurring cause.
Hours 22–24 dip: Response time dropped unusually low — even lower than overnight baseline. This could mean a maintenance window, traffic redirect, or a monitoring gap.

What This Activity Showed

✓

Before marking anything, engineers study the overall shape first to understand what "normal" looks like. You can't spot what's unusual until you know what usual is.

✓

Trends are predictable patterns — gradual changes that happen the same way repeatedly. Anomalies break that pattern. The same spike happening two days in a row at the same time is a pattern, not a coincidence.

✓

AI tools watch thousands of metrics simultaneously and surface anomalies the moment they appear. But humans still need pattern recognition skills to understand what the AI flagged and decide what to do about it.
