📋
Error Logs
System-written records of exactly what happened and when. Every line follows the same format. Great for debugging — not so easy to scan quickly.
🎫
Support Tickets
Written by people in their own words. Rich with context and emotion — but messy and hard to analyze at scale without help from AI or a human reader.
📊
Performance Data
Numbers tracked over time — response speed, uptime, error rates. Easy to visualize and spot trends in. The most AI-friendly type for catching patterns early.
The tricky ones — what to remember

Logs vs. Metrics: Logs record individual events ("this error happened at 9:14:03"). Metrics track patterns over time ("error rate was 12% last hour"). Logs can contain numbers — but if it's recording what a specific event was, it's a log.

Tickets with tech details: A support ticket is still a ticket even if it includes error codes or technical language. What makes it a ticket is that a person wrote it to describe their experience. The source and format are what define the type — not the vocabulary used.

Why This Matters for AI

✓

AI tools are trained to work with different data types in different ways. Knowing what kind of data you're looking at helps you pick the right tool and ask the right questions.

✓

Performance data is the easiest for AI to analyze — it's all numbers and patterns. Tickets are the hardest — all that human context and emotion needs interpretation before AI can do much with it.

✓

In a real incident, you'll often have all three types at once — logs telling you what happened, metrics showing how bad it got, and tickets telling you how it felt from the user's side. Together they give you the full picture.
