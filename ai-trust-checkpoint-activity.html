The Scenario: Automated Loan Pre-Screening
A bank has built an AI-powered workflow that automatically handles the first stage of every loan application. Read the full scenario before reviewing the steps.
LoanFlow AI — Automated Pre-Screening Pipeline · Production ● LIVE

What this workflow does: When someone applies for a personal loan online, this system automatically processes their application from start to finish — without any human involvement unless the system flags a problem.

The volume: This workflow runs about 400 times per day. A senior loan officer used to review each application manually. The bank automated the process to save time and handle more volume.

The stakes: Each decision affects whether a real person gets access to money — and potentially what interest rate they pay. Incorrect decisions can cause financial harm to applicants or create risk for the bank.

Your job: You've been brought in as a systems reviewer to look at this workflow before the next version goes live. You need to mark which steps are safe to run automatically — and which ones need a human to double-check the AI's work before anything happens.

💡 Think before you stamp: Not every step needs a human check — that would defeat the purpose of automation. Focus on the steps where a mistake would have the biggest consequence. Where could the AI be missing context? Where does a wrong output cause serious harm?

What This Activity Showed

✓

Not every AI step needs human review — that would defeat the purpose of automation. The goal is strategic placement of checkpoints where mistakes have the biggest consequences.

✓

Critical checkpoints usually fall where: AI is interpreting data (not just moving it), a decision affects a real person's outcome, or an error early in the process cascades into later steps.

✓

Safe-to-automate steps are usually execution steps — carrying out a confirmed decision, sending a notification, logging a record. The judgment comes before; the automation executes after.

✓

At scale, even a small AI error rate becomes a serious problem. 400 applications per day means a 1% flaw affects thousands of real people per year — which is why human checkpoints in high-volume automated systems are not optional.
