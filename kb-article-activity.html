Employees can't log in after company-wide password reset

What happened: At 9:15 AM, IT sent out a company-wide forced password reset. By 9:30 AM, the help desk started getting flooded with calls — employees were getting an error saying their new password "doesn't meet requirements" even when it did. Some employees were being locked out after 3 tries.

Who was affected: All 340 employees who tried to log in between 9:15 and 10:45 AM. About 180 of them couldn't get in at all.

What turned out to be wrong: The password reset system had an old rule still active that required passwords to be at least 12 characters. The company changed the rule to 10 characters six months ago — but nobody updated it in the reset system. So when people created new 10-character passwords, the system silently rejected them without explaining why.

How it was fixed: IT found the old rule at 10:20 AM by checking the reset system's settings. They updated it to match the current 10-character minimum. All locked-out accounts were unlocked by 10:45 AM. No data was lost.

● PUBLISHED — KB-0848 Today 11:43 AM · by You (IT Analyst)
Password reset fails with "doesn't meet requirements" error — employees get locked out
Password Reset Account Lockout Login Issues Company-Wide
🔴 The Problem
After a company-wide password reset, employees see a "password doesn't meet requirements" error even when their new password follows the rules. Repeated attempts can lock the account.
🔍 What Causes This
The password reset system had a minimum-length rule set to 12 characters. The company's actual policy is 10 characters, but the reset system setting was never updated to match.
🔧 How to Fix It
  1. Go to the password reset system settings and find the minimum password length rule. Change it from 12 to 10 characters to match current policy.
  2. Unlock all accounts that were locked during the incident.
  3. Confirm with test users that the new password reset process works correctly.
🛡️ How to Prevent This
Whenever any company-wide password policy changes, create a checklist of every system that stores or enforces password rules and update all of them before the change goes live. Add this checklist to the standard change management process.
